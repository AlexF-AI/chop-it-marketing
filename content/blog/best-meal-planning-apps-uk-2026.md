# Best Meal Planning Apps in the UK for 2026

*By [Alex Fahey](/author/alex-fahey), founder of Chop it. Last updated 5 August 2026. Every price below was checked on the UK App Store or inside the app on 5 August 2026.*

Start with Good Food if you want the largest bank of reliable British recipes. Buy Paprika 3 if you want to own your collection outright and never pay again. Use Samsung Food if you want a free planner that writes the shopping list for you across phone, tablet and web. Pick Cherrypick if you shop at Sainsbury's, Tesco or Asda and want your basket priced while you plan. Choose AnyList if the real problem is that four people in your house need the same list. And if your recipes arrive from ChatGPT one day and a cookbook the next, that scattering is the problem I built Chop it to solve.

We built Chop It. The other nine were assessed from their App Store listings, current pricing pages and published user reviews; Chop It was scored against the same criteria. I have not lived inside every competitor for a month, and I am not going to write as though I had.

## How I judged them

Anyone can win a features checklist. These apps separate on five things that only matter once you are three weeks in.

**Getting recipes in.** A built-in catalogue is one route. Clipping from a website is another. Photographing a cookbook page, saving a TikTok, keeping something ChatGPT wrote for you: those are the routes most apps still ignore.

**The shopping list.** Four recipes each want an onion. A good list gives you three onions on one line. A bad one gives you "1 onion" four times and lets you do the arithmetic in the shop. I have written more about [how each app handles that merge](/blog/meal-planning-app-shopping-list-uk).

**Pantry awareness.** You already own rice. An app that knows this writes a shorter list.

**Planning the week.** A calendar you fill in by hand is not planning. Look for leftovers, repeats and a view of the whole week at once.

**Price and platform.** A one-off £4.99 and a £59.99 annual subscription are different commitments. So is Android support, or the lack of it.

## The apps

### Chop it

This one is mine, so weigh it accordingly. I built it because a good dinner idea should not vanish when a chat window closes. Recipes arrive from everywhere now, and most apps only accept one or two of those routes. Chop it takes a website link, a photographed cookbook page, a social video or something you created in conversation, and turns each into a structured recipe in the same library. From there you plan the week, merge the ingredients into one list and cook from a clear method.

It also runs [inside ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com/plugins/plugin_asdk_app_6a1c0dfa60b88191b3360153a1c6613c), where you can find or create a recipe, build a menu and open the result in the app.

**The honest limitation:** it launched in May 2026 and there is no Android app. The built-in catalogue is smaller than a publisher with decades of archive. If you use Android or want the biggest editorial recipe bank, buy something else.

**Price:** free to start. Pro is £3.99 a month or £34.99 a year. Credit packs are £5.99 for 100 or £54.99 for 700.

### Mealime

Mealime does one job properly: choose a few quick dinners, get a clean aisle-sorted list, cook in around half an hour. The allergy and diet filtering is better than most, and the free tier is usable on its own rather than a two-week tease. For a household that wants dinner solved with minimal thought, it is the least demanding app here.

**The honest limitation:** it is a filtered database that never learns your habits. There is no meaningful calendar, the web version is close to read-only, and it does nothing with the food already in your kitchen. You will re-answer the same questions in month three that you answered in week one.

**Price:** free, with Meal Planner Pro at £2.99 a month. That is the cheapest paid tier in this comparison.

### Samsung Food

Formerly Whisk, and you do not need a Samsung device to use it. This is the most generous free tier of any app here. Save recipes from anywhere on the web, plan them onto a week, get a shopping list built for you, and do all of it on iOS, Android and a real web app. Most competitors charge for that combination.

**The honest limitation:** the product has grown broad, and finding the feature you want takes longer than it should. The best parts, including AI-personalised plans and the step-by-step cooking mode, sit behind a subscription that costs more per year than any other app in this table apart from Eat This Much.

**Price:** free core. Premium is £6.99 a month or £59.99 a year with a seven-day trial.

### Paprika 3

The recipe manager for people who want to own their collection. Clip from any site, scale the servings, generate a grocery list, sync between devices, and pay once. No renewal, no tier above you, no feature held back. Fifteen years of people migrating their recipes into it says something about how well it holds up.

**The honest limitation:** it organises what you bring, so it is a filing system rather than a source of ideas. "Pay once" means once per platform: the iPhone app and the Mac app are separate purchases. There is no pantry awareness and no supermarket connection.

**Price:** £4.99 on the UK App Store, one time. Desktop versions are sold separately.

### Cherrypick

The only app here that closes the loop between planning and paying. Choose your recipes and Cherrypick builds the basket at Sainsbury's, Tesco or Asda, with real prices attached, so you see the cost of the week before you commit to it. You can scan your fridge and cupboard so it skips what you already own. For anyone tracking a weekly grocery budget, that is a genuine advantage over every other app in this comparison.

**The honest limitation:** the retailer integration defines the product, so you are choosing among three supermarkets. Shop at Aldi, Lidl or Waitrose and the main reason to use it disappears. Some features are Sainsbury's only.

**Price:** free tier. Plus is £3.49 a month and Pro is £6.99 a month, both discounted 25% until 4 September 2026.

### Plan to Eat

A planner for people who already have the recipes and want somewhere disciplined to put them. The drag-and-drop calendar is the best in this group, the aisle-grouped list is well organised, and the web app is a full citizen rather than an afterthought. Households that cook from a fixed rotation of family recipes get on with it well.

**The honest limitation:** it is subscription-only after the trial, at £49.99 a year, with no free tier at all. It was built for a US audience, so ingredient names and quantities lean American and you will do some translating. There is no pantry awareness.

**Price:** £49.99 a year after a 14-day free trial. Nothing free beyond that.

### AnyList

If your actual problem is that your partner is in Tesco while you are at home looking at the fridge, AnyList solves it and nothing else here comes close. The shared list updates in real time, it is organised sensibly, and the household plan covers everyone in the house for £14.99 a year. Recipe storage and meal planning are present and competent.

**The honest limitation:** the list comes first and everything else runs thinner. As a place to discover recipes or plan an interesting week, it is outclassed by most of this list. You are buying a shared list with a planner attached.

**Price:** free core. Complete is £9.99 a year for one person or £14.99 a year for a household, the cheapest annual price here.

### Eat This Much

The pick if you are cooking to a number. Set a calorie target and macro split, and it builds a week that hits them, then produces the shopping list. Nothing else here automates against a numeric goal in the same way, and for anyone tracking intake for training or medical reasons, that automation is the whole point.

**The honest limitation:** planning to a target makes for clinical dinners. The recipes serve the numbers rather than the evening. It is also the most expensive option here by a distance, and the monthly price is roughly five times Mealime's.

**Price:** free basics. Premium is £14.99 a month or £58.99 a year after a 14-day trial.

### Good Food

The default for British home cooking, and the reason it stays the default is the archive. A large catalogue of triple-tested recipes from a brand most UK kitchens already trust, with browsing free and no account needed to start. If you want to type "chicken thighs" and get twenty things that will work, this is still the strongest answer.

**The honest limitation:** the meal planner sits behind Premium, so the free experience is a recipe bank rather than a planning tool. It does nothing with recipes from outside its own catalogue, which is most of them if you cook from social video or AI.

**Price:** free to browse. Premium is £4.49 a month or £24.99 a year, with a £0.99 annual trial offer running at the time of writing.

### Mob

The most distinctive recipe brand here. Strong originals, a clear point of view, and food that looks worth cooking on a Friday. For cooking as a pleasure rather than a logistics problem, Mob is the one that will actually make you want to get the pan out. Recipes are written for British kitchens and British ingredients.

**The honest limitation:** the full library and the meal plans are behind Premium at £39.99 a year, and it stays a curated publisher rather than a tool for managing your own collection. You cannot bring recipes in from elsewhere and organise them.

**Price:** free taster. Premium is £6.99 a month or £39.99 a year with a seven-day trial.

### ChatGPT on its own

Worth taking seriously as a competitor to every app above, because for a lot of people it already replaced one.

What it does better than anything on this list: improvising. Tell it you have half a cabbage, some feta and forty minutes, and it will give you something plausible to cook. Ask for the same dinner made cheaper, or without dairy, or for a child who refuses anything green, and it adapts without a filter menu. The free tier is enough for this. No app here matches that range.

What it cannot do on its own is hold anything. It forgets your week between sessions, cannot keep a shopping list you add to across days, and does not know what is in your cupboard. Ask it on Wednesday what you planned on Sunday and you will be retyping. That gap between generating a recipe and keeping one is [worth understanding properly](/blog/chatgpt-meal-planning) before you decide whether you need an app at all.

Chop it runs inside ChatGPT to close exactly those gaps.

**Price:** free tier is enough for meal planning. Go is £6.99 and Plus is £19.99 on the UK App Store.

## A note on meal kits

If you arrived here thinking of Gousto, HelloFresh or Mindful Chef, those solve a related problem at a different price. A two-person Gousto box works out at £4.65 a portion, £46.49 an order, before delivery. HelloFresh is £5.50 a serving, so three meals for two people comes to £32.99 plus £4.99 shipping. Both advertise a much lower price on a first box, which is not the number you pay in week three, so compare on the standard rate.

Kits take the deciding and the shopping off your hands, and you pay a premium per meal for that. The apps above take the deciding off your hands and leave you buying at supermarket prices, which is where most of the difference sits. I have written a fuller comparison of [what the kits cost against a planned week](/blog/hellofresh-alternatives-uk), including Mindful Chef at £7.62 a portion.

## What actually separates them

Most comparisons rank these apps by the size of the built-in recipe catalogue. That made sense when a publisher decided what you cooked. It matters less now that good recipes reach you from a cookbook, a friend's text, a saved video and a chat window in the same week.

The question worth asking is whether an app can turn all of those into something you can find again in March. Can that recipe join a weekly plan, become part of one merged shopping list and open cleanly at the hob, without you copying it between three tools? Paprika answers yes for anything on the web. Samsung Food answers yes for a good deal of it, free. Chop it is built for the case where the sources are genuinely mixed, including the ones that did not exist five years ago.

None of that helps if you stop opening the app. The best meal planning app is the one still on your home screen in week three, so match it to the job in front of you rather than the longest feature list. Browse a few [quick weeknight recipes](/recipes/collection/quick) or a [batch-cooking set](/recipes/collection/batch) and see which app you would actually want holding them.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best meal planner app in the UK?**
There is no single winner, because the apps solve different problems. Good Food has the best British recipe archive. Paprika 3 is the best way to own a collection without a subscription. Samsung Food is the strongest free planner. Cherrypick is the only one that prices your basket at a UK supermarket while you plan. Chop it is built for recipes arriving from mixed sources including AI.

**Is there a free meal planning app in the UK?**
Yes, several. Samsung Food has the most capable free tier, covering saving, planning and shopping lists across iOS, Android and web. Mealime's free tier is usable on its own. AnyList and Cherrypick both have free versions, Good Food is free to browse, and Chop it is free to start. Paprika 3 has no free tier because it is a £4.99 one-time purchase instead.

**Which meal planning app has the best shopping list?**
For a shared household list, AnyList. For a list that becomes an actual supermarket basket with prices, Cherrypick. For merging duplicate ingredients across several recipes into single lines, Chop it. Mealime and Plan to Eat both produce clean aisle-sorted lists from your plan.

**Can I use ChatGPT for meal planning instead of an app?**
For coming up with ideas, yes, and the free tier is enough. It struggles with continuity: it will not remember the week you planned on Sunday, cannot hold a running shopping list, and does not know what is in your cupboards. Either accept some retyping or use something that keeps the output.

**Which meal planning apps work on Android?**
Mealime, Samsung Food, Paprika 3, Cherrypick, Plan to Eat, AnyList, Eat This Much, Good Food and Mob all have Android apps. Chop it is iPhone-only at the moment, though the ChatGPT app works on any device.

**How much should I expect to pay?**
The cheapest paid tier here is Mealime at £2.99 a month. The cheapest annual commitment is AnyList at £9.99. Paprika 3 costs £4.99 once and never again. At the other end, Eat This Much is £14.99 a month and Samsung Food is £59.99 a year. Most people should start on a free tier and only pay once they have used an app for a fortnight.
